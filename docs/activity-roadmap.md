# Twenty activities: release scope and future work

The first-release scope is 20 unique activities in total. The earlier target of 50 is deferred. Prioritize polish, Montessori accuracy, clear guidance, and educator feedback before expanding the library. The six environments are Nido (0–18 months), Toddler (18 months–3 years), Children’s House (3–6), Lower Elementary (6–9), Upper Elementary (9–12), and Adolescents (12–18). Shared activities count once; not every environment requires an interactive exercise.

## Admission criteria

Each new activity needs:

1. An identifiable Montessori material or documented Montessori practice, supported by a specific source.
2. An educator-editable presentation, with the source distinguished from local adaptations.
3. The material’s recognizable shape, dimensions, arrangement, and defining action in the simulation.
4. A control of error appropriate to the work. Fixed-answer card ordering must not replace social practice, free artistic exploration, or adolescent project work.
5. A test of the learner interaction and visual inspection, separate from pedagogy review.

Source checks and availability decisions are recorded in `src/content/authenticity.ts`. An item marked `rebuild` or `unverified` cannot launch or appear as a classroom material. Prototypes that remain visible still disclose their limitations to adults.

## Sources and age-group distinctions

- [World Puzzle Map presentation, E&O Montessori](https://usa.montessorimaterials.com/articles/world-puzzle-map-presentation): globe-to-two-hemisphere presentation and continent inset handling.
- [AMI materials](https://montessori-ami.org/resource-library/materials): material standards and approved manufacturers.
- [AMI nomenclature](https://digital.montessori-ami.org/nomenclature): structured vocabulary and classification resources.
- [AMS Practical Life](https://amshq.org/blog/child-psychology/2023-10-23-practical-life-through-the-ages-part-one-early-childhood/): purposeful everyday work, including serving and table preparation.
- [AMI adolescent environment](https://montessori-ami.org/node/8509): adolescent education has no fixed set of specialized Montessori materials. Document actual community practices rather than inventing a preschool-style catalog.

Nido content should explain and demonstrate the prepared environment to adults. It must not imply that infants need to complete screen exercises. Adolescent project completion must not claim mastery of a real occupation or social practice.
